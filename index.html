Health Data Reimagined: The OMOP CDM Architecture

Transforming Fragmented Hospital Records into Verifiable Global Evidence

Author

Harish Mohankumar

Translating the Chaos of Clinical Data

Clinical records live in hundreds of incompatible dialects across thousands of hospitals. By engineering an OMOP Common Data Model foundation, we turned disconnected patient records into reproducible clinical insights.

Lead Engineer: Harish Mohankumar | Platform: OMOP CDM v5.4, PostgreSQL 16, OHDSI ATLAS / WebAPI 2.13.0, HADES (R)

1. The Multi-Billion-Dollar Translation Problem

Every clinic, health system, and diagnostic laboratory records patient care differently.

When a clinician in Boston writes that a patient has “Type 2 Diabetes,” the electronic health record might store it as an internal billing code like ICD-10 E11.9. In London, the same condition is logged as an NHS read-code. In Munich, it lives inside an unstructured clinical narrative.

The raw data exists, but the systems cannot talk to each other.

When medical leaders need to answer an urgent question, such as “Does Medication A protect diabetic patients from kidney failure better than Medication B?” data engineers spend months per site manually cleaning files and reconciling custom tables. By the time results arrive, budgets are spent and clinical opportunities are lost.

2. The Solution: The Shipping Container Analogy

Before 1956, international shipping was slow, fragile, and inefficient. Goods arrived at ports in wooden barrels, woven sacks, and custom crates. Hundreds of dockworkers spent days unpacking and restowing cargo by hand for every unique ship hold.

Global trade changed forever with a single innovation: the intermodal shipping container.

It does not matter whether a container holds electronics, automotive parts, or fresh fruit. Because the dimensions, steel castings, and crane locking mechanisms are standardized worldwide, any ship can carry it, any crane can hoist it, and any train can haul it without opening the doors.

The OMOP Common Data Model (CDM) is that shipping container for health data.

Maintained by the international OHDSI (Observational Health Data Sciences and Informatics) open-science community, OMOP achieves two foundational goals:

  1. Structural Standardization: Every patient, encounter, prescription, diagnosis, and lab result lives in the exact same table with identical column names, everywhere in the world.
  2. Semantic Standardization: Messy local billing codes and proprietary brand names are mapped into a single, unambiguous concept ID. “Glucophage,” “Metformin Hydrochloride,” and international brand names all map to concept ID 1503297.

3. The Pipeline: From Raw Data to Causal Answers

  1. Raw Records: Hospital claims, doctor visits, and lab panels arrive in disparate formats.
  2. ETL Cleansing: Data pipelines validate date chronologies, remove duplicates, and structure inputs.
  3. Vocabulary Harmonization: Standard medical dictionaries (Athena) map raw strings to certified clinical concepts.
  4. The OMOP Database: Data is persisted in a relational store (PostgreSQL) governed by standard integrity rules.
  5. Reproducible Analytics: Standardized HADES statistical packages evaluate causal questions and safety metrics.

5. Business Payoff

Months to Hours

Time-to-Evidence Acceleration

Once health data is transformed to the OMOP CDM, running new epidemiological studies does not require custom SQL engineering. Validated network packages run in hours rather than months.

  • Collaborative Research Without Data Transfer: Institutions join international multi-center studies with millions of patient records without sharing sensitive patient-level rows, fully respecting GDPR and HIPAA boundaries.
  • Audit-Proof Data Governance: Automated validation via the OHDSI Data Quality Dashboard (DQD) enforces thousands of consistency, plausibility, and completeness checks.
  • Reusable Analytical Assets: Every trial emulation, cohort phenotype, and survival analysis script functions as version-controlled code that runs on future database refreshes automatically.
